# Process log: Rick and Morty 3D

The 3D remake of the 2D RPG in ~/rick-platformer. One entry per session. The 2D game's own log stays in its folder.

### Session 1, 2026-11-12
- Goal: Pitch the 3D version, then build the first playable piece: Smith garage hub, movement, film camera, dialogue, portal effect, cold open.
- AI types and tools: code AI (Claude Code, Claude Fable 5.2) for design, code and testing. No new image or audio AI this session: characters, portraits, music and sound effects are the Gemini and ElevenLabs outputs from the 2D game, re-cut for 3D.
- Key prompts (word for word):
  - "Build a 3D version of my Rick and Morty RPG, on steroids. You own the design and the build. Surprise me."
  - "Cinematic feel: lighting, effects, camera, transitions. The game should feel like an episode of the show."
  - "Start with the Smith garage hub, one portal puzzle area, and the boss. Add areas only once those three feel great."
  - "Go, build it"
- What was built:
  - New project in ~/rick-3d: Three.js 0.187 + Vite, vanilla JavaScript. Packages: three, vite. Nothing else.
  - Garage set built from boxes and cylinders to match the painted 2D garage (shelf, red tool cabinet, pegboard, garage door, jar shelves, workbench with humming tubes, cardboard boxes, paint cans). Two-tone cartoon shading, dark outlines on every prop, real shadows, glow on the tubes and jars.
  - Morty and Rick as paper cutouts that turn to face the camera. Morty: 8-direction movement with smooth speed-up, walk bob, lean, flip, squash on stop. Rick lies passed out with floating Z's.
  - Film camera: follows Morty in play; in dialogue it cuts to an over-the-shoulder shot of whoever speaks and slowly pushes in. Letterbox bars, screen shake, flash, fades.
  - Dialogue box with big portrait, typewriter text, choices, story flags. Same JSON format as the 2D game.
  - Green portal: swirl drawn by a small shader, wobbly rim, orbiting sparks, green light.
  - Cold open (about 25 seconds, Escape skips): portal rips open, Rick falls out spinning, the gun bounces and cracks, three glowing parts fly back into the portal, Rick says one line, Morty walks in, title card.
  - Playable loop: talk to Rick, check the gun, pull the trigger, the gun fires a portal, walk in, "Next: Shrimp Rick's lab" card, back to the garage.
- Key decisions:
  - First choice: paper cutouts in 3D sets instead of 3D character models, to reuse the graded Gemini art. Rodrigo rejected this after playing it (see the revision below).
  - Story compressed for the 3-area slice: all 3 gun parts land in Shrimp Rick's lab (the 2D game spreads them over 3 areas). Rick's "where" line and the gun screen line were rewritten for this. Two new lines in the cold open: Rick "Nailed it. Zzzz." and Morty "Rick? What was that noise?"
  - Dialogue box is drawn by the browser (HTML) on top of the 3D picture, so text and portraits stay sharp.
  - Outlines are a second, slightly bigger copy of each shape drawn inside out in near-black. Cheap, and works on a slow laptop.
  - Cutscenes are written with await, one line per beat, and Escape fast-forwards time 40x instead of needing separate skip code.
- What went wrong or looked weak, and the revision:
  - Sprites from the 2D game were 144 px tall and blurry in 3D. Re-cut Morty and Rick from the original Gemini sheets at full size (210 px sprites, 340 px portraits) with a copy of tools/cut_sheet.py.
  - Cutouts had a white halo (pale pixels between the white page and the ink line). Added a 3-pass fringe trim to the cutter.
  - First render was too bright and the gun light blew the floor out to white. Cut every light to about a third, darker blue fill light.
  - Dialogue shots v1 were too close: characters filled half the screen and looked blurry, and Morty walked through Rick. Camera pulled back from 6.2 to 9 units, added a collision box for Rick and the gun.
  - Portal v1 was a solid white blob. Portal brightness 1.6 to 1.2, glow strength 0.85 to 0.5, so only the hot bands glow.
  - Title card v1 landed on top of Morty's face. It now cuts to a high wide shot of the garage first.
  - The preview pane runs the game in slow motion, so key-hold tests did nothing. Added a dev-only __game.step() hook to run frames by hand.
- Revision after Rodrigo played the first build: "i dont like the paper 3d, switch to low poly"
  - Morty, Rick and the portal gun are now low-poly 3D models built in code (src/objects/models.js): faceted shapes, same two-tone shading and outlines as the set. No model files and no new image AI. The 2D Gemini portraits stay in the dialogue box only.
  - Real walk cycle: legs and arms swing, the body leans and turns to face where it walks, slow breathing when standing.
  - Jars, tubes and paint cans in the set got flat faces too (8 sides) so the whole room matches.
  - Low-poly v1 looked weak: faces sat in the dark shadow tone, eyes were the same color as skin, Rick lying flat on his back was an unreadable pile, the gun stood on its barrel and its glow washed it out.
  - Low-poly v2: characters get a lighter shadow tone and unlit white eyes, Rick now sits slumped against the workbench with his head hanging (he lifts it only for his own lines), he lands sitting after two backflips, the gun stands on its handle and its light moved up.
  - Dialogue camera moved back in from 9 to 6.8 units, because 3D models stay sharp up close (the paper cutouts did not).
- Rejected, and why:
  - Paper cutout characters (my first design): Rodrigo did not like the look. Kept in git history and in the screenshots as the before.
  - Sculpted or downloaded character models: no time, and model files from the internet would not be our own work.
  - Outline as a screen effect (edge detection): draws boxes around the flat cutouts and costs a second render of the scene.
  - Extra packages (animation library, post-processing library): Three.js already ships bloom, and a 40-line tween helper covers the animation.
  - A separate area for each gun part: the brief is 3 great areas first.
- Result: Playable from start card to the portal exit, no console errors, production build passes. Deployed to GitHub Pages at https://rodrigocaceres-coder.github.io/rick-3d/ (new public repo rodrigocaceres-coder/rick-3d, a GitHub Actions workflow rebuilds it on every push to main). Letterbox bars and real-time feel are not yet confirmed on Rodrigo's machine, because the preview pane throttles animation.

  - Shrimp Rick entrance v1: the camera was behind the barrier wall. v2: it sat inside a character's head. v3 uses fixed cameras for that corner.

  - Crates and beams going through portals (from the pitch): cut to keep three rooms finished and tested by Nov 17.
  - A timer in room 3: the guard crossing the firing lane already gives the pressure.
- Result: Playable from the start card through all 3 lab rooms to the "Next: the boss fight" card, tested room by room and garage-to-lab with stepped frames, no console errors, production build passes. ### Session 3, 2026-11-12
- Goal: Build slice 3: the turn-based boss fight vs Shrimp Rick from STORY.md, staged with camera cuts and a timed Space press.
- AI types and tools: code AI (Claude Code, Claude Fable 5.2) for design, code and testing. No new image or audio AI: portraits, music (boss_phase1, boss_phase2, ending) and sound effects are the Gemini and ElevenLabs outputs from the 2D game. The tank is a low-poly model built in code.
- Key prompts (word for word):
  - "Read CLAUDE.md and PROCESS.md, then build step 3: the turn-based boss fight vs Shrimp Rick from ~/rick-platformer/STORY.md, staged with camera cuts and a timed Space press. Log it as Session 3. you have plenty of creative freedom to make this great"
- What was built:
  - The fight happens in the Smith garage. The lab ending no longer shows a "to be continued" card: Morty tumbles home through the portal, Shrimp Rick walks out after him, a "FINAL BOSS: SHRIMP RICK" card slams in. Rick sleeps through the whole fight in the background.
  - Rules from STORY.md in src/systems/battleRules.js (numbers only, no drawing): Morty 60 HP, Shrimp Rick 120 HP, Shoot 10 + 3 per gun part, Item, Talk, Defend, phase 2 at 60 HP, Portal Banish at 15 HP or less.
  - Timed Space press: a green ring shrinks onto a white ring. On Shoot: PERFECT is 1.5x damage, NICE is normal, MISS is 0.6x. On his attacks: pressing as the hit lands blocks half of it.
  - Phase 1 (on foot): Claw Snap (he runs at Morty), Shell Up (a shell bubble blocks the next shot), Sauce Spray (red splats on the camera lens, the next ring is faster and swims around).
  - Phase 2: he whistles, a portal opens in the ceiling, his walking fishbowl tank drops out, he jumps in. Phase 2 music. Tidal Slam is announced a turn ahead ("He's winding up! DEFEND!"). Defending opens a portal shield that swallows the wave. Krill Swarm is 3 hits, each with its own block ring.
  - Talk: mentioning Prawnsworth's rebels halves his damage for 2 turns (only if Morty helped Prawnsworth in the lab). In phase 2 Morty's honest plea makes him lose a turn. Otherwise a joke reply.
  - Items: Mom's sandwich (+25 HP) and arcade soda (+15 HP, Morty moves again).
  - Portal Banish finisher, the ending from STORY.md (Rick: "See, Morty? I handled it."), an end card, and a two-line post-credits gag.
  - Camera: a different shot for the menu, aiming, each attack, every hit and every line of dialogue. HP bars with status tags, attack name banners, pop-up damage numbers.
  - Losing shows a "TRY AGAIN" card and restarts the phase you lost in, with items back.
  - All fight text is in src/data/dialogue/boss.json and src/data/items.json. Test links: ?boss (whole fight), ?boss=fight (skip the opening), ?boss=2 (tank phase), add &norebels to play without Prawnsworth's help.
- Key decisions:
  - Fight in the garage, not the lab: Portal Banish "sends him home", so he has to be away from home. It also reuses the best-looking set and the sleeping Rick joke.
  - Sauce Spray changed from "50% chance to miss" (STORY.md) to a harder timing ring where a miss means the shot goes wide. Skill instead of a coin flip.
  - A normal shot can never take him below 1 HP, so every win ends with the Portal Banish scene.
  - The timing ring runs on the game clock, not a browser animation, so test scripts can step through it frame by frame.
- What went wrong or looked weak, and the revision:
  - Wide shot v1: the cardboard boxes along the front of the garage filled the bottom third of the picture. Camera raised from 1.9 to 3.4 so it looks over them.
  - Tidal Slam camera v1 sat under the landing tank, so the tank filled the screen and Morty was not in the shot. v2 is a low shot from behind Morty. In v2 the shield portal was side-on, a thin sliver. v3 turns the portal toward the camera.
  - Krill Swarm camera v1 was stuck behind the tank's claw. Moved behind Morty so the krill fly at the camera.
  - Tank phase was too short: in the first full test run he entered the tank at 44 HP and one charged shot took him to 6. No Krill Swarm was ever seen. Now the tank water heals him back to 60 when he climbs in, and he always sends krill right after a slam.
  - He used Shell Up 3 times in 5 turns, and Sauce Spray twice in a row. Now he never repeats either one two turns running.
  - The menu remembered the last pick, so after Defend the cursor sat on Defend when Portal Banish came up. It now always starts on Shoot.
  - My first test script stepped the game 60 frames in one go and nothing happened: cutscene code only continues between frames. Test scripts now step one frame at a time.
- Rejected, and why:
  - A separate boss arena: a new set would not be finished and tested by Nov 17.
  - The 7 boss items from the 2D game: the 3D slice has no house or arcade to find them in. Kept the two items STORY.md names.
  - Remembering the last menu pick (see above).
- Result: Tested with a script that plays the game: lab room 3 through the whole fight to the ending and back to the garage, no console errors, production build passes. Loss and retry tested. 5000 simulated fights per play style: shooting only with no blocks and no items wins 36%, using the two items wins 100%, an average player (60% good timing, half the blocks) wins 96% in about 9 turns. Not confirmed on Rodrigo's machine: real-time feel of the timing ring, and the letterbox bars (the preview pane throttles animation). Not deployed yet.
